PIVOT APPAREL DESIGN TOOL
Initial Brief for Grill Me
Purpose

Pivot intends to develop a digital apparel design tool that enables sporting clubs to create coordinated, customised teamwear through a simple, intuitive and controlled design experience.

The tool should reduce the complexity traditionally associated with custom teamwear design while helping ensure designs meet applicable sport, league, competition, product and manufacturing requirements.

The target customer is the sporting club, rather than the individual consumer.

Core Principles
Start with the Club

The club's identity should be the starting point for the design process, rather than a blank garment or a generic professional-team design.

The design process should use the club's:

logo;
colours;
branding;
selected sport;
selected products;
design requirements; and
applicable compliance requirements.

For onboarded clubs, approved club assets should already be available within the tool.

Do Not Make Clubs Start from Scratch

The tool should provide suitable starting design options based on:

the club's logo and colours;
available patterns;
the selected garment template;
the elements required on the garment; and
applicable design and compliance constraints.

The customer should be able to select a preferred starting point and customise it.

Compliance by Design

Pivot will maintain relevant sport and league design requirements for the Greater Bendigo sporting environment and incorporate these requirements into the design process.

Where practical, the tool should prevent non-compliant designs from being created rather than relying solely on checking completed designs afterwards.

Examples may include:

number size;
number placement;
permitted number formats;
approved fonts where applicable;
logo and sponsor placement;
minimum clearances;
garment safety zones;
colour and visibility requirements;
sport or competition-specific requirements; and
product and manufacturing constraints.

Mandatory elements should be pre-positioned, locked or constrained where appropriate.

Explain Restrictions

When the tool prevents, restricts or warns against an action, it should explain why.

For example:

This number cannot be made smaller because the applicable competition rules require a minimum number size.

Where practical, the tool should also provide an immediate corrective option.

Validation should distinguish between:

Blocking errors — the design cannot proceed;
Warnings — the design may proceed, but the customer should understand the issue; and
Manual review items — Pivot review is required.
Code-First Automation

Where a function can be reliably performed using deterministic code, configured rules or structured data, Pivot should use that approach rather than AI.

AI should only be considered where it provides material value that cannot reasonably be achieved through conventional software.

This principle is intended to support predictable performance, lower variable operating costs, easier testing and reduced dependency on external AI services.

Demonstrate Value Before Asking for the Lead

Prospective clubs should be able to experience the genuine Pivot design tool before being required to create an account or provide contact information.

The customer should be able to begin designing without:

forced account creation;
an immediate sales enquiry;
intrusive pop-ups;
automatic chatbot interruptions; or
a compulsory onboarding tour.

A natural lead-capture point is when the customer chooses to save their design.

Key Terminology
Garment Template

The physical garment shape or cut and its defined editable areas.

For example, different basketball jersey cuts or neckline styles would be different garment templates.

Pattern

The visual graphic or pattern applied to the garment.

Design Elements

Elements placed on or incorporated into the garment, such as:

club logo;
club name;
sponsor logos;
player name; and
player numbers.
Proposed Design Journey
1. Choose Sport

The customer selects their sporting code.

For the Phoenix pilot, the initial sport is basketball.

2. Choose Products

The club selects the products it wants to design as part of its range.

Examples may include:

playing jersey;
playing shorts;
club tee;
training top;
warm-up apparel; and
other available teamwear.

The objective is to design a coordinated club range, rather than requiring each product to be designed independently from scratch.

3. Establish Club Identity

The customer uploads or selects the club logo.

The tool should support establishing the club's actual colour palette using precise colour information where available.

The uploaded logo may be used to identify or suggest colours as a starting point, with the customer able to confirm or change them.

For an onboarded club, approved logos and colours should already be available.

4. Choose the Primary Garment Template

The customer selects from the available garment shapes or templates.

For basketball, the primary garment would initially be the playing jersey.

Where multiple garment cuts or styles are available from the approved supplier, the customer should be able to choose between them.

5. Confirm What Needs to Appear on the Garment

Before starting designs are presented, the tool should establish which design elements need to be accommodated.

For a basketball jersey, this may include:

☑ Player number — required
☐ Player name
☐ Club name or text
☐ Sponsor logo or logos

Other mandatory elements may be automatically included where required by the applicable sport, competition or league rules.

Mandatory requirements should be pre-selected and unable to be removed, with an explanation available as to why they are required.

The purpose of this step is to ensure the starting designs already account for the elements that need to appear on the garment.

6. Receive Starting Design Options

The tool should present several suitable starting designs based on:

club logo;
club colours;
selected garment template;
selected design elements;
available patterns; and
applicable design and compliance constraints.

The customer should not be required to begin with a blank garment.

The starting point should be the club's identity, not a generic professional team design.

How these starting options are generated without becoming repetitive remains to be explored through Grill Me and subsequent technical design.

7. Design the Primary Garment

The customer selects a starting design and customises the permitted elements.

Potential controls include:

garment colours;
individual garment components;
trim;
panels;
patterns;
club logo;
club name or text;
sponsor logos;
player names;
numbers; and
other product-specific options.

The design should update visually as changes are made.

The customer should have meaningful creative control. Where a choice is likely to produce a poor visual outcome, the preferred approach is generally to warn and explain, rather than unnecessarily prevent the customer from making the choice.

Compliance and production constraints may require some elements to be locked or restricted.

Player numbers are expected to be locked or constrained in accordance with applicable requirements.

Suggested positions may be provided for elements such as club logos, club names and sponsor logos, while still allowing appropriate customer control.

8. Approve the Core Design Direction

The primary garment establishes the core design direction for the broader apparel range.

The exact point at which the design becomes formally approved, including the relationship between digital approval and physical sample approval, remains to be resolved.

9. Pre-Populate the Remaining Selected Products

The tool should apply the approved core design direction to the other products selected by the club.

This may include:

colour palette;
pattern or visual direction;
club branding;
sponsor hierarchy; and
other reusable design characteristics.

The system should not simply copy one garment design onto another. It should apply defined rules appropriate to each product and garment template.

The club can then review and refine each product.

10. Review and Submit

The completed range is reviewed and submitted into the applicable Pivot approval process before production.

The exact approval and production workflow will depend partly on the selected manufacturing partner.

Design Experience Requirements

The design interface should prioritise:

a large, clear live product preview;
easy navigation between garment components;
clear identification of the element currently being edited;
easy access to available patterns;
visible and understandable colour controls;
precise colour entry where supported;
saved club colours;
the ability to replace repeated uses of one colour with another;
clear front, back and other required garment views;
contextual guidance;
fast loading; and
responsive interaction.

Where a sport, product, garment or design option is displayed as a visual card, the entire card should be selectable, rather than requiring the customer to locate a small button.

The tool should teach through the experience rather than requiring customers to learn the software before using it.

Colour Management

The tool should support clubs using their actual brand colours rather than restricting them to an unexplained generic palette.

Potential capabilities include:

saved club colours;
precise colour entry;
manufacturer-supported colour references;
recently used colours;
colours suggested from uploaded club branding; and
replacement of all instances of one design colour with another.

Where a selected colour cannot be produced or creates a compliance or visibility issue, the tool should explain the limitation or warning.

The method for translating club colours into manufacturer production colours remains dependent on supplier capabilities and requires further information.

Existing club garments may potentially provide a colour reference, subject to the selected manufacturer's colour-matching process.

Design and Compliance Validation

The tool should incorporate automated validation where practical.

Potential validation areas include:

uploaded artwork file type;
image quality and resolution;
artwork dimensions and scalability;
placement within approved zones;
distance from seams, cut lines and restricted areas;
number and name sizing;
logo and sponsor sizing;
sport and competition requirements;
colour and visibility;
product-specific manufacturing requirements; and
completeness of required information.

Where a requirement can be expressed as a fixed rule, it should preferably be enforced through code.

Human review may remain necessary for quality assurance, exceptions and requirements that cannot be reliably automated.

Reversible Garments

Where a reversible garment is selected, the reverse side should be treated as an intentionally designed light alternative, rather than automatically becoming a plain white garment with a logo and number placed onto it.

The light alternative should:

remain visually connected to the primary design;
meet applicable light/dark uniform requirements;
retain the club's identity; and
appear deliberately designed.

The exact design logic for reversible garments remains to be explored.

Coordinated Range Design

The system should treat the selected products as a coordinated apparel range.

The club should establish its core design direction once and then refine the application of that direction across individual products.

This is intended to reduce duplicated design effort and create a more consistent club identity across playing, training and club apparel.

Prospective and Onboarded Club Experience

The same core design experience should support both prospective and onboarded clubs.

Public access should demonstrate the genuine quality and ease of the Pivot design experience rather than provide a materially reduced or inferior demonstration.

Prospective users should be able to begin designing without creating an account.

When a prospective customer chooses to save their design, Pivot may request minimal information such as:

name;
club name; and
email address.

The design can then be stored and accessed again through an account or secure link.

The precise save and lead-capture journey remains to be refined, but the principle is:

Let the customer experience the value of the tool before asking for their details.

Following club onboarding and authentication, additional club-specific functionality may become available, including:

approved club assets;
saved club colours;
saved and approved designs;
applicable sport and competition requirements;
selected products;
design history; and
other club administration functions.
Access and Administration

The anticipated access model may include:

Club administrator — manages the club's Pivot account and club-controlled functions.
Authorised design users — potentially one or two users delegated by the club administrator.
Pivot administrator — manages the platform and intervenes where required.

Pivot does not manage the club's online store on the club's behalf.

The exact roles and permissions remain to be resolved.

Help and Guidance

The tool should be simple enough that most users can use it without assistance.

Help should nevertheless be available when required.

Potential support options include:

a simple step-by-step guide;
contextual help for more complex controls;
clear explanations for restrictions and warnings; and
an option to request further help.

Help should be user-initiated.

It should not:

automatically pop up;
interrupt the design process;
launch an unsolicited chatbot; or
require the customer to complete a tutorial before designing.
Design Changes and Version Management

Approved designs may need to change between seasons or before a future order window opens.

The system should support future design changes while protecting active commercial and production processes from uncontrolled changes.

A design should not be changed in a way that affects an active order or production process.

The exact rules governing:

when designs can be changed;
whether changes are restricted around order windows;
how redesign work is commercially managed;
design versioning; and
archiving previous approved designs

remain to be resolved.

Approved and historical designs may be stored within the club's design or asset library.

Supplier Dependency

The target design tool and automated operating model depend on integration with an approved manufacturing partner.

Supplier integration capability is assessed separately through Pivot's Supplier Evaluation process.

The design tool should remain supplier-agnostic where practical and support integration with the selected manufacturing partner through a viable and sufficiently automated integration pathway.

Supplier-dependent matters include:

available garment templates;
manufacturing constraints;
colour matching;
production file requirements;
artwork specifications;
order data requirements; and
integration methods.
Outputs

The final required system outputs are not yet determined.

Potential outputs may include:

customer design preview;
saved editable design;
structured design data;
approval proof;
design specification;
production artwork;
manufacturing files or data; and
integration payloads for the selected manufacturing partner.

Final output requirements will depend partly on the selected supplier's production and integration processes.

Pilot Delivery Approach

The Phoenix pilot is currently targeted for mid-August 2026.

The Pivot design tool should only be used for the pilot if it is fully functioning and reliable for the required pilot scope.

Time is currently the primary factor determining whether Pivot's own tool can be used.

If the Pivot design tool is not ready, the pilot may require:

an approved white-label or third-party design solution; or
an interim process that enables the pilot to proceed while the permanent Pivot tool continues to be developed.

The interim solution should not define or unnecessarily constrain the architecture of the permanent Pivot design tool.
