**Status:** Research and controlled intake — no asset approved or added to the Studio

**Research date:** 5 August 2026

**Owner:** Director

## 1. Decision summary

No candidate currently satisfies Pivot's complete approval gate, but one has now passed controlled intake. Exact AIC files were assessed sequentially with dated rights evidence, source checksums and full-resolution inspection:

1. *Botanical Specimen* was rejected because its subject is extremely faint and inseparable;
2. *Roses in a Vase* was rejected because the flowers and vase are indistinct within a dense tonal field; and
3. *Panel (Furnishing Fabric)* is the **first suitable candidate**. Its butterfly border is clear, decorative, low-risk and technically promising for a full-colour rectangular placement or border.

The panel remains **Pending**, unavailable in the Studio and prohibited for manufacture until its external capture margin is removed in a controlled derivative, the derivative is checksummed and print-tested, the supplier accepts it and the Director approves the exact files and uses. No candidate has been placed under `public/` or added to the Studio.

*Fragment* remains an uninspected reserve candidate. Hokusai's *Wave* remains second-line pending stronger Met image-specific rights evidence.

This is an operational rights screen, not legal advice. Ambiguous cultural, trademark, personality or supplier questions require appropriate specialist or legal review before Director approval.

## 2. Governing constraints applied

- Pivot controls artwork and release to manufacture independently of any club approval.
- Public availability, a download control, public-domain age or a collection-wide policy does not approve an exact file.
- Public assets must not include real club assets or broaden use rights for the Pivot penguin.
- The initial library should favour clear, practical, reusable artwork suitable for community and junior sport.
- No institution, artist or collection may be presented as endorsing Pivot, a club or a garment.
- Exact source files, evidence, transformations, approvals and released production versions must remain auditable.

## 5. Cross-cutting rights and suitability findings

### Copyright and licence

- AIC provides the clearest reviewed combination of exact object-level designation and commercial-use policy.
- CC0 does not grant rights the affirmer does not hold and does not clear trademarks, privacy, publicity, moral rights, cultural authority or misleading conduct.
- “Any purpose” supports merchandise printing in ordinary licence interpretation, but Pivot should record that conclusion for the exact file and intended commercial garment uses rather than expect a policy to name every product category.
- No shortlisted asset has an identified recurring royalty, territory limit or end date. This must be rechecked when the exact file is acquired.

### Cultural and community-sport suitability

- Prefer botanical and European decorative assets with low apparent cultural or personality risk for the first intake.
- Historical Asian works may still be suitable, but require accurate naming and contextual review rather than being treated as generic mascot art.
- Do not use museum artefacts, national emblems, sacred imagery or Indigenous designs merely because copyright has expired.
- None of the shortlist should be described as Australian native, club heritage, official, authentic cultural artwork or institution-approved without separate evidence.
- Final visual inspection must screen for text, seals, crests, restoration targets, collection labels and symbols not apparent in metadata.

### Technical production

- The inspected AIC panel has enough pixels for a moderate border placement, not automatically for all-over sublimation.
- JPEG source files have no transparency. Every background-removed file is a derivative requiring its own filename, checksum, treatment record and approval scope.
- Fine charcoal, woodblock and textile detail can close up, break or produce haloes. Test on light and dark garments and against supplier colour profiles.
- Safe vectorisation means tracing only the verified public-domain artwork, recording software and settings, preserving a visual comparison and manually removing introduced paths or artefacts. It does not mean the resulting file is an exact historical reproduction.
- Do not use AI upscaling, generative fill, automated restoration or generative vectorisation in the initial intake. If later approved, identify the tool/model, date, prompts/settings, output checksum and human comparison, and reassess introduced content and rights.
- Supplier sign-off must cover format, colour space, bleed, minimum line/knockout size, effective resolution, transparency, placement and print method before production approval.

## 7. Director approval checklist

For each exact asset and each separately controlled production derivative:

- [ ] The register ID, exact title, creator, date, institution, accession/object ID and authoritative object URL match the source.
- [ ] The exact source file has been retained outside the public application path.
- [ ] Download timestamp, final response URL, filename, MIME type, byte size, pixel dimensions, colour profile and SHA-256 are recorded.
- [ ] A dated PDF, HTML capture or other immutable capture shows the exact object's rights designation.
- [ ] The applicable licence/public-domain statement and terms have been retained with the source.
- [ ] Underlying-work rights and digital-file rights have both been assessed.
- [ ] Commercial Studio display, customer modification, derivative creation and garment/merchandise printing are expressly supported by the evidence.
- [ ] Attribution and notice obligations are recorded and implemented.
- [ ] Territory, duration, fee and high-resolution-file differences have been checked.
- [ ] Trademark, logo, publicity, privacy, moral-rights and design-right risks are acceptable.
- [ ] Cultural sensitivity, sacred/restricted material, ICIP, community custodianship and contextual integrity have been reviewed.
- [ ] Content is suitable for community and junior sport and does not imply sponsorship, affiliation, competition approval or cultural authority.
- [ ] Full-resolution visual inspection has found no hidden marks, labels, colour targets, signatures or problematic restoration artefacts.
- [ ] Print size, method, colour, transparency, line detail, crop and light/dark garment behaviour have passed supplier review or a documented supplier specification.
- [ ] Every derivative records source checksum, treatment, software, operator, date, output checksum and visual QA result.
- [ ] Studio previews cannot substitute an unapproved or later source revision.
- [ ] The immutable manufacturing package contains only the approved derivative/version.
- [ ] Review triggers include source/terms change, supplier change, new print method, new transformation, cultural concern or complaint.
- [ ] The Director approves the exact checksums, permitted transformations, Studio scope and production uses in a controlled record.
- [ ] The Image Register has been updated only after that decision.

## 8. Attribution handling recommendation

1. Keep attribution metadata attached to every asset even when CC0 makes display optional.
2. Show a compact **Artwork details** panel in the Studio with creator, title, date, source institution, object link, rights label and “No endorsement implied”.
3. Include the same data in the saved design manifest and internal approval record.
4. Do not print institutional attribution on the garment unless the exact licence requires it or the Director expressly approves it; visible museum naming can be mistaken for sponsorship.
5. For AIC candidates that pass intake, use the requested caption format in Studio details and records, for example: `Printed and manufactured by Lancaster Prints. Panel (Furnishing Fabric), 1856. The Art Institute of Chicago.`
6. Where a derivative is shown, add `Adapted by Pivot from…` and name the operations without claiming the altered file is the museum's reproduction.
7. Link the exact licence/version where applicable, but do not display an AIC, Met, Getty or Creative Commons logo without checking applicable trademark guidelines.

## 9. Source and derivative storage recommendation

Use controlled, non-public storage with immutable versions; do not put evidence or source masters under `public/`.

Suggested record layout (logical rather than a required repository structure):

```text
image-assets/<register-id>/
  evidence/
    object-page-<date>.pdf-or-html
    rights-policy-<date>.pdf-or-html
    evidence-manifest.json
  source/
    <unchanged-source-filename>
  derivatives/
    <derivative-id>/
      production-file
      treatment.json
      visual-comparison
  approvals/
    director-decision
    supplier-review
```

The evidence manifest should contain source and final URLs, retrieval timestamp, HTTP content type and byte size, SHA-256, dimensions, rights quotation and URL, creator metadata, permitted uses, attribution, risk decisions and review triggers. Store derivatives by content hash or immutable version. The Studio should publish only the specifically approved delivery derivative, while approval and manufacture records retain its source relationship for the required record period.

Recommended naming:

```text
PIR-008__aic-1958.762__source__sha256-<prefix>.jpg
PIR-008-D01__external-margin-crop__sha256-<prefix>.png
PIR-008-P01__studio-preview__sha256-<prefix>.jpg
```

Never replace a source or derivative in place. A changed crop, recolour, trace, cleanup or output profile receives a new derivative ID and checksum.
